Photo Club Hub

What might come next

Photo Club Hub shows the photographs that members of photography clubs choose to show. Multiple clubs in one place, so a visitor does not have to find each club’s website and learn how to navigate every single site. A companion program turns the same information into ordinary web pages that a club can put on its existing site.

Not come across the app yet? There are really two versions, with much the same content: the app in the App Store for Apple devices, and a web version for anything with a browser, Android and Windows included: www.fcdegender.nl/hub/en/clubs.

The list below is what features might be added next — possibilities, not promises. What actually gets built depends largely on what people consider necessary, how much work each one is, and other practical matters.

One thing worth knowing before reading it: the app already knows about far more clubs than have supplied any photographs. Almost every club listed has an empty page waiting for it. That shapes the whole list — a good deal of what follows is about making it easier for a club to take part.

Note that a second list (not shown) exists with internal improvements. These are harder to explain and judge.

Each item has been estimated for how much work it would be, but those estimates are deliberately not shown here. Seeing them would only push everyone towards the cheap ones, and what is needed is what you actually want. A cost/benefit judgement comes afterwards.

How to reply

Pick the three you would most like to see, and send back their numbers in order of preference — for example 4, 9, 1. The order matters: your first choice gets more points than your third choice.

Three rather than five because people are usually sure about their first two or three, and after that mostly fill something in because “it has to be done”. Just reply to the email this was attached to. If it reached you some other way, the address is vdhamer@msn.com.

Getting more members covered

1
A place for individual members
Individual members, who are not attached to a club, can find each other too.
Started
2
Every Dutch club on the map
Find your club already on the map wherever it is in the country, rather than only clubs in a couple of regions.
3
Edit club list in a form
Clubs in a region are entered using simple forms that are already largely pre-filled. You only need to check them and add any missing bits.
4
Edit club data in a form
Your club’s members and portfolios are entered using simple forms that are already largely pre-filled. You only need to check them and add any missing bits.

Finding what interests you

5
A screen for expertises
Browse the full list of photography types the way the website already allows.
6
Search on more than names
Search for a club or a kind of photography, not only for a photographer’s name.
7
Show me what I care about
Tell the app which clubs, people and kinds of photography interest you. It then opens on those, and can let you know when they have something new.

Enjoying portfolios of others

8
Photos from past exhibitions
See the work a club showed at an exhibition, after the exhibition has ended.
Started
9
A better way to look at the photographs
More of the photographs, and shown better. This one asks whether the area is worth our effort, before anyone decides what it should look like.

The bigger picture

10
Exhibitions near you
See which club exhibitions are coming near you soon. So you never miss one that you wanted to attend.
11
Watch the project grow
See how many clubs, photographers and photos the app covers, and how that is changing.

Keeping it working

12
Noticing when someone joins or leaves
When someone joins or leaves your club, the club’s own website usually says so first. The app can notice this by itself.

Two more open questions

Is something missing? These are twelve things somebody already thought of. If what you actually want is not among them, saying so is worth more than any ranking, because no amount of ranking can reveal it.

You decide how much weight it carries. If it belongs in your three, put it there — reply with something like “first: being able to print a club’s photos, which is not listed, then 4, 9”. Something worth displacing one of the twelve is exactly what we hope to learn from the answers. If it is more of a nice-to-have, just mention it and leave your three as they are.

Anything else at all? Including “this will not work, because…”. Doubts about the whole idea, or about how it is being approached, are worth more than encouragement and are much harder to come by. Nobody minds being told the plan is wrong while it is still cheap to change.
